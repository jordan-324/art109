bio

Jordan Medina is a multimedia artist from the San Francisco Bay Area. He is currently working toward a Bachelor in Fine Arts in Digital Media Arts at San Jose State University. Medina works across various mediums ranging from painting, photography, video, installation and digital processes. His practice seeks to document, question, and preserve. Medina’s work is a conversation between place, memory, and the strange intersections of human life.

statement

I am a multimedia artist from San José, California. My work spans video, digital collage, photography, installation, and painting. It is rooted in the experiences of working-class communities and explores the intersections of technology, memory, and class, reflecting on how digital culture shapes the way we see and remember the world around us.

I witnessed firsthand the coexistence of working-class life and the rapid expansion of Silicon Valley growing up in San José. This environment sparked a fascination with the tensions between innovation and everyday survival, where progress and displacement exist side by side. My work often focuses on capturing overlooked spaces and reimagining the stories they hold.

The work reflects on the cultural and psychological effects of experiencing life through data. Surveillance appears throughout my practice as both a system of control and a normalized part of daily life that shapes identity and perception. I often blend humor, nostalgia, and paranoia as a subtle critique of the awkward translation between digital life and human experience.

At its core, my practice seeks to document, question, and preserve. I combine empathy, critique, and humor. For me, art means sharing and experimenting with ideas, stories, and histories through different mediums. I believe the best work opens up space for interpretation and discussion. My practice invites viewers to reflect on the intersections of community, technology, and memory—capturing the strange and beautiful conditions of everyday life.
